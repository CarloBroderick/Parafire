Notas
  • Leñosas cerradas → TU5. Scott & Burgan 2005 (RMRS-GTR-153) TU5 = very high load, dry-climate timber-shrub: heavy forest litter under a shrub or small-tree understory carries the fire (moderate spread, moderate flame length). This fits Chaco dry forest (quebrachal), which has a dense thorny shrub layer, terrestrial bromeliads (caraguatá) and deep deciduous litter in the dry season. The pilot areas are Chaco, so V01 picks the Chaco reading. The class also covers humid Atlantic (Alto Paraná) forest, where TU5 overstates fire behaviour.
  • Pastura → GR2. Cultivated forage pasture (in the Chaco: gatton panic, buffel grass, Brachiaria), usually grazed. S&B GR2 = low-load dry-climate grass: grass is the carrier, shrubs (if any) do not affect behaviour, high spread rate, moderate flame length. Grazing keeps the load low. This class is sensitive: ungrazed gatton panic can carry far more fuel.
  • Leñosas inundables → TU3. The official definition covers trees, shrubs and palm savannas (palmares, e.g. karanday) that depend on flooding. S&B TU3 = moderate-load humid-climate timber-grass-shrub: forest litter with grass and shrub components and high extinction moisture (30 %), high spread rate, moderate flame length. That combination of grass layer, woody component and humid climate fits.
  • Pastizales inundables → GR6. Esteros, bañados and cañadas: natural herbaceous vegetation that depends on flooding. S&B GR6 = moderate-load humid-climate grass (fuelbed depth 1.5 ft, extinction moisture 40 %; very high spread rate, high flame length). The humid-climate grass models fit vegetation that only burns once the wetland dries out. A moderate load is chosen because the class also includes short wet meadows.
  • Pastizal → GR4. Natural grassland (Chaco espartillares and pajonales, campos) where woody cover is under ~20 %. S&B GR4 = moderate-load dry-climate grass: continuous, coarse grass about 2 ft deep, high spread rate, moderate flame length. Grass is the primary carrier.
  • Leñosas abiertas → GS2. With 15–65 % woody canopy, enough light reaches the ground for grass and shrubs to carry the fire. S&B GS2 = moderate-load dry-climate grass-shrub: shrubs 1–3 ft with moderate grass, high spread rate, moderate flame length. This matches Chaco and Cerrado savanna woodland and open quebrachal.
  • Forestaciones → TL6. Tree plantations, mostly eucalyptus in the Región Oriental, plus pine and fruit or citrus orchards. S&B TL6 = moderate-load broadleaf litter (less compact than TL2), moderate spread rate, low flame length. Eucalyptus leaf and bark litter is the main surface carrier in managed stands.
  • Borrador técnico (V01 DRAFT); espera el análisis y la validación del equipo de combustibles.

Notas
  • Agricultura → NB3. Annual grain and fibre crops (soy, maize, wheat). S&B NB3 = agricultural field maintained in a non-burnable condition. Green crops and tilled fields act as firebreaks in spread simulation. Alternativas: GR1 (101, short sparse dry-climate grass) for post-harvest stubble in the dry season; GR2 (102) for fallow fields. Consider a seasonal rule in V02.
  • Ríos, lagos y océano → NB8. Open water. S&B NB8 = open water (non-burnable). The engine's source comment labels code 98 'NB4'; Scott & Burgan call it NB8. Alternativas: None.
  • Áreas sin vegetación → NB9. Built-up areas, roads, quarries and natural bare surfaces (outcrops, sand banks, salt flats). The S&B NB codes are behaviourally identical in the engine: all are non-burnable with zero load. NB9 (bare ground) is picked because in the Chaco most class-25 pixels are roads, bare soil and salt flats. The engine's source comment labels code 99 'NB5'; Scott & Burgan call it NB9. Alternativas: NB1 (91, urban/developed) for towns. Behaviour is identical; it only changes the label.
  • Para la V02: regla estacional para agricultura (GR1 o GR2 en rastrojo, NB3 con cultivo verde).

This fits Chaco dry forest (quebrachal), which has a dense thorny shrub layer, terrestrial bromeliads (caraguatá) and deep deciduous litter in the dry season. The pilot areas are Chaco, so V01 picks the Chaco reading. The class also covers humid Atlantic (Alto Paraná) forest, where TU5 overstates fire behaviour.

That combination of grass layer, woody component and humid climate fits.
  • Flooded grasslands → GR6. Esteros, bañados and cañadas: natural herbaceous vegetation that depends on flooding. S&B GR6 = moderate-load humid-climate grass (fuelbed depth 1.5 ft, extinction moisture 40 %; very high spread rate, high flame length). The humid-climate grass models fit vegetation that only burns once the wetland dries out. A moderate load is chosen because the class also includes short wet meadows.
  • Grassland → GR4. Natural grassland (Chaco espartillares and pajonales, campos) where woody cover is under ~20 %. S&B GR4 = moderate-load dry-climate grass: continuous, coarse grass about 2 ft deep, high spread rate, moderate flame length. Grass is the primary carrier.

S&B GS2 = moderate-load dry-climate grass-shrub: shrubs 1–3 ft with moderate grass, high spread rate, moderate flame length. This matches Chaco and Cerrado savanna woodland and open quebrachal.
  • Forest Plantation → TL6. Tree plantations, mostly eucalyptus in the Región Oriental, plus pine and fruit or citrus orchards. S&B TL6 = moderate-load broadleaf litter (less compact than TL2), moderate spread rate, low flame length. Eucalyptus leaf and bark litter is the main surface carrier in managed stands.
